Plato's φάρμακον in translation

In the Phaedrus (274c–275b) the god Theuth offers writing to King Thamus as a pharmakon of memory and wisdom. Thamus answers that it is a pharmakon not of memory but of reminding, and that it will make people seem wise instead of wise. The word means remedy, drug and poison at once, so every translator has to choose. This page lays out what translators chose, across .
