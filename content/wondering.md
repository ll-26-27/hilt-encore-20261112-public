---
title: What we learned, what we're wondering
subtitle: Things we learned from teaching faculty to build with AI, and the questions it has left us with. Tell us yours.
---

## Learned

- Personalized learning materials emerged in the first week, unplanned.
- The first time through anything, you're designing it as much as delivering it.
- The tool changed faster than our materials: the desktop app went from useless to the only way we taught.
- What held people back was procedural knowledge: copying a path, the structure of their files.
- Even people who write code aren't necessarily at home in the command line.
- Finishing an activity isn't the same as seeing how it helps you; recipes made from people's own use cases did that.
- Presenting something once isn't knowing it. Practice is what's needed.
- Getting to excellent work takes skill and steering, not one-shot prompting.
- From week two to week eight, students went from "why web tools?" to building their own podcast-recording software.
- The oral exam is how you show you learned, and how you learn.

## Wondering

- What's still difficult? What's still scarce?
- The just-in-time, disposable app: what does it mean when no one shares the same tool?
- There's no message board for the app you vibe coded. What happens to tool-based knowledge?
- Is learning a tool worth it if it changes before the learning pays off? For an 18-year-old?
- If everyone uses AI, are we all leveled up and back where we started?
- Which skills no longer matter, and can students move straight to the harder questions?
- Learning objectives are moving targets: what is there to get better at?
- We've built more than ever. Have we learned more than ever?
