title: What is the Learning Lab?
question: What do we do, and why were we doing all this AI stuff?
who: Marlon
minutes: 10
---

The Learning Lab is a studio and a team at the Bok Center. What we do is some research, but mostly technical innovations, pedagogical innovations, and things that are marvelous aesthetically.

Before November 2022 our tools were the tools of the creative industries: Adobe Creative Suite, Blender, Unreal, Unity, Ableton, Logic, GarageBand; Blackmagic cameras and ATEM switchers in the studio; Python, Node.js, and Bash for command-line tools; Next.js and Express for servers that switched cameras and routed signals live. We edit videos of instructors teaching, videos for courses and student projects, and promotional videos.

We also do a lot of knowledge work. We track everything: all the video, all the projects, all the students who work here. That gets us into databases, with Airtable as a human-readable layer and SQL for little apps. We've built web apps for courses, and the most ambitious graduate fellows learned to build full-stack apps for classes themselves.

When we consult on an assignment, our job is to understand the course well enough to talk with the professor about what's in alignment with their learning objectives. We read the syllabus, and also the professor's own books and papers, to understand how they think about the subject, not just the topic. We rarely recommend open-ended "creative projects." We look for the places where a video essay or a web-based essay serves the learning objectives better than the conventional essay: academic work, made with creative tools.

That comes from our debt to the Writing Program. Writing is deeply meaningful: students aren't just showing you that they learned, they're developing their learning through the process of writing. It's very uncertain whether they're doing that when they use AI.

On any given day, working here means a little lifting and building, a little reading Hegel, a little writing JavaScript, a little operating a camera or a microphone, performing, editing. AI has made all of those things easy. That raises the question of what our edge is. But for the last couple of years, this intersection of skills has given us a useful vantage point from which to watch the whole generative AI phenomenon, and the futures it could bring.

We've always had to learn the next new thing: a 3D modeling app, a video editing app, a new phone. In a sense AI was just the next new thing. Except that it makes it easier to learn it and everything else, and it carries the threat that learning it won't matter, because everything will soon be too easy.
