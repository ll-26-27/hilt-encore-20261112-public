---
title: What we learned
question: What changed in our work, and in our class?
who: Madeleine, then Marlon
minutes: 13
---

Personalized learning materials weren't in the plan. We started making them in the first week, for people for whom things weren't yet concrete. We made them first for faculty; what's exciting is that we can now make them for students as well. Madeleine's individual and small-group consultations grew out of this. The argument we want to make is for scalable personalized learning.

It changed our own workflow: internal media production tools we build for ourselves; new modes of collaboration on knowledge work, where sharing context is the key move; and, ultimately, our class.

In TDM 155AI this term, the class has a context pack, so sessions can be organic while students still have all the material available to them. Students give each other their context folders to understand how to work together. In week one or two, it may not make sense why we spend so much time learning to make little web tools. By week eight they're building their own podcast-recording software, free and better for them than GarageBand or Logic, and making tools for their other courses. They can make tools fitted to their project, or to their own needs, for accessibility or other reasons.

Students do almost constant oral reflection on what they're doing. When they code with Claude Code, every step is in the git history and the chat: a complete record they can turn in. In the past you had to condense the process into a product for evaluation; it's not clear we always need to. Where the summative artifact matters, the capstone or the academic essay, keep it. But the oral exam is how you make sure you learned, and also how you learn. The gap between formative and summative starts to close.

We've built more than ever. Have we learned more than ever?
