Have AI portray each student (in a story, an image, a song) from their own words, and ask them to name exactly where it gets them wrong.

## How it runs

Students give the AI a short self-description, get a portrait back, and write or say three divergences: what it invented, what it smoothed over, what it made generic. Share a few. Close with what this shows about AI output on subjects where you are not the expert.

## With Claude

In Claude Desktop with the self-portrait file attached: "Write a one-page origin story of me in the style of a superhero comic." Then: "List every claim in that story that isn't supported by my file." Compare Claude's list with the student's.

## Why it works

People see errors about themselves quickly and precisely, so the exercise trains the habit of checking AI output against what you know, and shows how forms and genres distort.

## Time and materials

Twenty minutes; Claude Desktop.

## Variations

Portray the student's hometown, family recipe, or research project; portray the instructor first as a demonstration. Run it before students judge AI output on course material: they are the experts on themselves, so they can name every distortion before they are expert enough to name the field's.
