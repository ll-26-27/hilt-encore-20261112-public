Ask early how and where someone will encounter each project, and before the end, plan each one's place in the actual space.

## How it runs

When projects begin, each student answers: what will it produce, and how and where will someone encounter it? Midway, each makes a quick spatial prototype or site plan. Near the end, the class walks the real room together and assigns walls, screens, and stations, deciding what must run unattended.

## With Claude

Have Claude Code build a floor plan page from a photo or sketch of the room where students drag their project onto a spot and list its needs (power, sound, screen, supervision); export the list for the setup crew.

## Why it works

"Installation cannot first appear in the final class": thinking about encounter early changes what students make, and planning in the real room surfaces problems while they can still be solved.

## Time and materials

15 minutes early; 30 minutes midway; 60 minutes near the end. The real space.

## Variations

A poster session; a reading; a website's information architecture.
