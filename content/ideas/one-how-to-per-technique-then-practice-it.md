For each technique a project needs, students ask the AI to write a step-by-step how-to, then practice the technique from it and correct the how-to where it was wrong.

## How it runs

Students list the techniques their project requires. For each, they get a how-to from the AI, saved as a file. In the next session they practice from the how-to and mark it up: what worked, what was missing, what was wrong. The corrected how-tos become their personal manual.

## With Claude

In Claude Code: "Write a how-to for <technique> in docs/how-to/<slug>.md: what it is, what you need, the steps, three ways it goes wrong, and an exercise I can repeat five times." After practice: "Here are my notes from trying it; update the how-to."

## Why it works

It turns AI explanations into something tested against practice, and gives students a record of what they learned by doing.

## Time and materials

Twenty minutes to generate; a practice session.

## Variations

Share how-tos across the class; compare two students' corrected versions of the same technique.
