On the first day, students write a self-portrait in words (who they are, what they want to make, how they like to work) and save it as the instruction file their AI tools read in every session.

## How it runs

Pairs interview each other for five minutes each way and take notes. Each student turns the notes into a one-page self-portrait. They save it as `CLAUDE.md` (or the equivalent project instructions) at the root of their working folder. All term, they revise it as they learn what helps.

## With Claude

In Claude Code, a `CLAUDE.md` at the folder root is read at the start of every session; in Claude Desktop, paste it into a Project's instructions. Ask Claude: "Read my CLAUDE.md and tell me three things you'll do differently because of it," and see whether it does.

## Why it works

It teaches context engineering before the term is named, makes the student the expert on the input, and gives them a working document that changes how the tools behave from day one.

## Time and materials

Thirty minutes in class; a laptop with Claude Desktop or Claude Code.

## Variations

Write it for a character, a lab persona, or a historical figure; compare two classmates' AIs answering the same question.
