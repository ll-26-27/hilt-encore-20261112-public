Teach interviewers that "the best question is usually the second one," with a short ladder of follow-ups that get at judgment.

## How it runs

Before peer interviews, give hosts three follow-ups to reach for: "What did you throw out, and why?" "How did you know that wasn't good enough?" "What can you do now that you couldn't at the start?" In practice rounds, hosts must ask at least one follow-up to every answer.

## With Claude

Have Claude play a guest who answers vaguely ("Be a student describing their project in general terms; only give specifics when I ask a good follow-up"), so hosts can practice climbing the ladder before the real interview.

## Why it works

First answers are rehearsed; follow-ups reach what the guest knows, and the ladder's questions ask for the judgment AI can't supply.

## Time and materials

15 minutes. A chat account for practice.

## Variations

Oral exams; job interview practice; thesis defenses.
