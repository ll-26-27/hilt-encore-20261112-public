Pairs browse a big reference map of the field looking only for things that surprise them, then report the one that changed their mind.

## How it runs

One partner drives, both talk. For 20 minutes they look for counterintuitive items (an unexpected leader, a gap, a number that doesn't fit their sense of the field). Each pair reports one surprise and what assumption it overturned.

## With Claude

Optional: after the pair picks a surprise, ask Claude to find the original source behind that row and say whether it supports the claim.

## Why it works

Looking for surprises makes students read a reference actively, and it gives them a feel for the whole field quickly. Reporting one surprise aloud forces them to name the belief it changed.

## Time and materials

25 minutes. Any large reference (the list map, a database, a statistical yearbook).

## Variations

Census tables; a museum's collection database; a corpus concordance.
