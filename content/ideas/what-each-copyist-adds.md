Restore a process to its separate stages so students can see what each stage adds, and then compare it with what an AI pipeline adds.

## How it runs

Pass an image (or text) through a chain of stages, one person or device per stage: a lens projects onto frosted acrylic, a phone photographs the acrylic, a projector shows the photo, a camera shoots the projection. At the end, compare the first and last versions and list what each stage added (vignette, grain, inversion, ghost). Then discuss: what does each step of an AI pipeline add?

## With Claude

Optional: run a text through a chain of model calls (summarize, translate, translate back, expand), saving each step, and ask Claude Code to build a page that shows the chain side by side with differences highlighted.

## Why it works

Seeing a process split into stages makes the artifacts of each stage visible; "noise is a resource," and the marks a chain leaves can become the aesthetic, as craft traditions have long said.

## Time and materials

30 minutes. A dark space or box, a lens, acrylic, phones, a projector; or just a chain of prompts.

## Variations

A telephone game with handwritten copies; translation chains in a language course; data passed through successive cleaning steps.
