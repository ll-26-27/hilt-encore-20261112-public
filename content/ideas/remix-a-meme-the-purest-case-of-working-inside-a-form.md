Students remix a famous meme template with AI tools, keeping the form and changing the content, then ask what had to survive for it to stay recognizable.

## How it runs

Give students a page of 20 famous templates with the images embedded. Each picks one and makes a series of remixes, from literal to inventive, using the template image as a reference. They post the series with the prompts and models used, and answer: what has to survive for this to still be that meme?

## With Claude

Ask Claude Code for a page of templates "sorted by how each one works rather than what it is about" (three roles, two panels, escalation), and a script that sends a template image as a reference with a prompt to an image model and saves the result with its prompt. Students run it in a loop, feeding one result into the next.

## Why it works

A meme is a fixed form with variable content, "the equivalent of the sonnet form," so remixing one gives the intuition of working within and against a form's constraints in a small, fast exercise.

## Time and materials

40 minutes. An image model (chat app or API).

## Variations

Rewrite a fable in a fixed structure; recompose a famous photograph; a set of variations on a musical phrase.
