Assign some work by time, not by product: a set number of minutes of practice, after which you are done, so students practice regularly instead of cramming.

## How it runs

Name the practice (sketching, an instrument, a lab technique, a writing exercise) and the time (for example, thirty uninterrupted minutes a week). Students log the session (start time, what they did, one observation) and stop when the time is up; going over is allowed but not required. Grade the log and the account, not the artifact.

## With Claude

Students dictate a thirty-second log into their phone after each session; a small script built with Claude Code transcribes the logs and appends them to one Markdown file per student with dates. Ask for "a script that watches a folder for audio files, transcribes each, and appends date + transcript to practice-log.md."

## Why it works

Time-boxed practice resembles training for a sport, not an all-nighter; the ceiling removes the open-ended anxiety, and the floor makes repetition happen.

## Time and materials

Twenty to thirty minutes a week outside class; a phone.

## Variations

Physical, device-free practices (drawing, handwriting, printmaking) as a counterweight to screen work; a shared class practice hour. At the start of a block, ask Claude for three ways to push the activity further; pick one and say in the reflection which and why.
