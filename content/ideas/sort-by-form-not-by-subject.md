Take a pile of examples that platforms sort by topic and re-sort them by form, so students see that the form is what they will learn to make.

## How it runs

Give students 20–40 examples sorted the usual way (by subject: sports, cooking, news). In groups they re-sort by form (how-to, recap, interview, commentary, review) and find a form that appears under several subjects. Discuss which form they'd want to learn.

## With Claude

Ask Claude Code to tag each row of a list with its form: "For each item in `examples.csv`, add a `form` column (how-to, recap, interview, commentary, review, other) and list every subject each form appears under." Students check and correct the tags.

## Why it works

"'Sports' is a subject; a match recap, an instructional video, and a sports interview are different forms." Separating form from content is what lets a student choose something to practice.

## Time and materials

30 minutes. A list of examples.

## Variations

Genres of academic writing (the review, the response, the case study) across disciplines; forms of scientific figures across fields.
