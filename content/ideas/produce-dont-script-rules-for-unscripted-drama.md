Instead of writing what AI characters say, students design the rules, roles, and twists that make an unscripted ensemble produce drama, as a reality-show producer would.

## How it runs

Students take a cast, then add producer mechanics one at a time: hidden roles, private confessionals, votes, a twist at a set turn, observers who comment. After each run they say what the new rule did to the conversation.

## With Claude

In Claude Code, add files to a cast system: `roles/traitor.md`, `producers/story-producer.md`, `observers/control-room.md`, and a rule in `system.md` ("confessionals after each round"). Then ask Claude to "run it, and show public lines and private confessionals in two columns."

## Why it works

The ensemble is "unscripted in that you don't give the characters words to say… But it is directed." Students learn orchestration as setting conditions, which is how directors, game designers, and teachers work.

## Time and materials

45 minutes. Claude Code; an API key.

## Variations

A mock trial with hidden evidence; a negotiation with secret goals; a class simulation of a historical summit.
