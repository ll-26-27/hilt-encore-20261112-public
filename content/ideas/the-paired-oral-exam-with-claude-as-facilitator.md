Two students sit with Claude, which asks one of them about the readings and their work while the other acts as an ally, so the exam feels like a conversation rather than a trial.

## How it runs

Pair students. Claude (voice mode or text) is given the readings and one student's work and asks that student questions, quoting short passages. The partner can help think aloud but not answer for them. Switch after fifteen minutes. The instructor listens or reviews the recording.

## With Claude

In Claude Desktop, create a Project with the readings and each student's work as files and a system prompt: "You are facilitating a paired oral exam. Ask one question at a time about how this student's work connects to the readings. Quote a verbatim passage of one or two sentences in each question and ask them to close-read it. Never answer for them." Use voice mode in the room. Mark quoted passages clearly so students know only the verbatim middle is reliable (see "The quote sandwich").

## Why it works

The AI takes the instructor's evaluative pressure off the room, the partner turns it into a dialogue, and the student still has to think in real time about real texts.

## Time and materials

Thirty minutes per pair; a laptop with voice mode; the readings as files.

## Variations

Groups of three; use it as review before a real exam; let pairs choose which reading.
