Take a professional tool students might learn (an editor, a design suite, a lab package) and map its pipeline: inputs, operations, interface, outputs, data, then mark which steps AI could do and which need the person.

## How it runs

In groups, students diagram one app's workflow from capture to delivery. For each step they note what it does and whether AI or a small custom tool could do it, and what a person must still decide. Groups present their maps; the class compares.

## With Claude

Ask Claude for the app's documented feature list, then ask it to build a tiny version of one step as a web page or script (for example, "a page that trims a video between two timestamps with ffmpeg in the browser"), to test the claim.

## Why it works

It moves students from tool loyalty to understanding what tools do, which transfers when the tools change.

## Time and materials

One session; access to the app's documentation.

## Variations

A statistics package in social science; a reference manager in history.
