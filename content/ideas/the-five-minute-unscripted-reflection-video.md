After each class, every student records at least five minutes of themselves talking, unscripted and unedited, about what they did, what stuck, and the choices they made.

## How it runs

Within a day of class, each student picks up a phone (front or rear camera, anywhere: walking, in the studio, at home) and talks for at least five minutes about three things: what they did in class, one thing that stuck with them, and the interesting choices they made in the piece they turned in. No preparation, no script, no retakes, no editing. They post the video beside the work in the class's shared channel. The instructor watches a sample each week and answers a few on camera.

## With Claude

Students drop the video's transcript into Claude Desktop and ask it for the three things they said that they didn't say in writing, and one question to start next week's video with. Or build a small Claude Code tool that transcribes each week's video into the student's own notebook folder, so by the end of term the reflections are a searchable record they can draw on for a final oral reflection. Another version (WK): Build with Claude Code a small script that transcribes each posted video (a speech-to-text API) and appends the transcript to a per-student Markdown log. At the end of the term, a student opens their own log in Claude and asks: "Read my reflections in order. Where did my vocabulary change? What did I say I couldn't do in the first month that I now describe doing?" The student checks every claim against the transcripts.

## Why it works

Talking unscripted forces the student to retrieve and connect what they did without a model writing it for them, so the video is evidence of their own thinking when AI use is everywhere else. Repeated weekly, it trains the skill of speaking about one's own work off the top of one's head.

## Time and materials

Five to ten minutes a week outside class; a phone; a shared channel or folder.

## Variations

Audio only for students uncomfortable on camera; a walking version ("talk while you walk" sounds less read); pairs who interview each other for five minutes instead; a lighter three-minute version early in term.
