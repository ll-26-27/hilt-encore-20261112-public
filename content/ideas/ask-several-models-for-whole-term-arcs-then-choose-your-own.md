When designing a course, have two or three AI models each propose complete semester structures, read them all, and then write your own shape.

## How it runs

Write a one-page brief: the course description, the number of sessions, the constraints, what you want each week to feel like. Give it to several models and ask each for three to five complete arcs, each built on one organizing idea. Read them; grade them against your program's goals; note what you like and reject. Then write your own structure and keep the arcs as a quarry of mechanics.

## With Claude

In Claude Code, put the brief, the catalog description, and example syllabi in a folder, and ask: "Write five complete 12-session arcs for this course, each as its own Markdown file with a one-paragraph thesis and one entry per session; then write a table grading all of them against the program description." Run the same brief through another model for contrast.

## Why it works

Seeing many coherent alternatives quickly makes your own priorities clear; the choice stays human, and the rejected arcs leave reusable pieces.

## Time and materials

An afternoon; access to two models.

## Variations

Do it for one unit; do it with students for their own project timelines.
