During a conversation, an interview, or a group discussion, a panel shows how long each person has spoken, live, and everyone looks at it afterward.

## How it runs

Run a ten-minute discussion with the talk-time panel on the projector (or only on the facilitator's screen). Afterward, compare the shares with what people expected and discuss what changed when they could see it.

## With Claude

Ask Claude Code: "From the per-speaker utterances in this transcript tool, keep a running total of seconds per speaker and show it as bars, with the share of the last two minutes beside the total." With one microphone per person, the split is reliable; with one shared microphone, ask for diarization in the transcription instead.

## Why it works

People misjudge their own share of a conversation; seeing it makes interviewers talk less and quiet students get invited in.

## Time and materials

15 minutes; an interview tool with speaker labels.

## Variations

Seminar discussions; team meetings in project courses; language classes where students aim for an equal share.
