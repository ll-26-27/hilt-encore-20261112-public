Before choosing a project, each student collects at least twenty examples of the form they might make, annotated with what to take from each.

## How it runs

Students start a list in class and finish it that week: twenty or more references (videos, pieces, papers) in one form, each with one line on what they'd emulate. They post it and talk about the patterns in their weekly reflection. The list becomes the raw material for choosing a project.

## With Claude

Ask Claude to interview the student about their list ("Ask me what I like about each, one at a time"), then write a one-page summary of the patterns, which the student corrects. Or in Claude Code, keep the list as `references.md` and have Claude build a page that embeds each one.

## Why it works

Collecting examples of a form is how practitioners learn its conventions; annotating them makes the student say what they are looking for.

## Time and materials

15 minutes in class; an hour outside.

## Variations

An annotated bibliography; a mood board; a set of model solutions to study.
