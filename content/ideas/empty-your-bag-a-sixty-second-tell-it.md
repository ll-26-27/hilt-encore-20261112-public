Students empty their bag, choose a rule for what to show, lay out the objects, and explain the selection to camera in sixty seconds.

## How it runs

First shoot the "honest dump" from above. Then choose a selection rule (what I carry; what I'd choose; what I want you to think I carry) and 6–12 objects including one "reveal." Lay them out square, light and photograph them, write numbered captions saying what each object lets you do, then record a 60-second "tell it" to camera, one sentence per object, ending "that's everything."

## With Claude

Optional: ask an image model "what's in the bag of [a one-line description of the student]?" and compare its guess with the real layout ("What did it assume you carry?"). The main activity needs no AI.

## Why it works

Talking about your own objects is the lowest-stakes way into speaking on camera, and connecting a set of objects aloud is a small oral exam in making sense of a collection.

## Time and materials

30 minutes. A table, a light, a phone or camera.

## Variations

Objects from a lab bench, a field kit, an archive box, or a historical figure's possessions; five objects from a museum gallery. Photograph the table and ask Claude to describe the objects and guess what they say about the owner; compare with the student's own account.
