Commission illustrated research essays on the history of the week's practice, label every one as AI-generated, and use them as discussion material rather than authority.

## How it runs

Choose a topic close to the week's practice (the history of notebooks, of rehearsal, of editing). Have the AI write short illustrated essays with sources and a 150-word label each. Print some for the walls or post them. Students pick one, check two of its claims against the cited sources, and report what held up.

## With Claude

In Claude Code: "Write eight short essays in `readings/` on the history of note-taking (one each on [topics]), each with a 150-word wall label, sources with links, and frontmatter `author: claude`; then build a page that shows an 'AI-generated' badge on every one." Use parallel subagents for the essays.

## Why it works

Students get rich context fast, and checking a labeled AI essay against its sources teaches them to treat AI research as a lead, not a finding.

## Time and materials

An hour to generate; 20 minutes in class. Claude Code; printer optional.

## Variations

A gallery of historical examples of the week's practice; a timeline; one essay per student to verify.
