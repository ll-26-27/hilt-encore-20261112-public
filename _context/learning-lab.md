# The Learning Lab

The Learning Lab is a studio and a team at the Derek Bok Center for Teaching and Learning, Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences. We do some research, but mostly we make things: technical innovations, pedagogical innovations, and things that are marvelous aesthetically.

- Write to us: learninglab@fas.harvard.edu

## What we make

Before generative AI, our tools were the tools of the creative industries: video and audio editing, 3D and game engines, cameras and switchers in a studio, and code (Python, Node.js, the command line, web servers) to tie them together. We film and edit instructors teaching, make video for courses and student projects, and build web apps for classes.

We also do a lot of knowledge work. We track every project, every video, and every student who works with us, which takes us into databases and small apps built on them.

## How we work with courses

When we consult on an assignment, our job is to understand the course well enough to talk with the instructor about what fits their learning objectives. We read the syllabus, and also the instructor's own writing, to understand how they think about the subject. We rarely recommend open-ended "creative projects." We look for the places where a video essay or a web-based essay serves the objectives better than the conventional essay: academic work, made with creative tools.

That comes from our debt to the Writing Program. In writing, students aren't only showing that they learned; they learn by writing. Whether they still do that when they write with AI is an open question for us.

## How we work with AI

- **Context first.** Each project has a folder of context (plans, sources, notes, decisions) that a person or an agent can read to pick it up. We share context the way we used to share files.
- **Small tools, made for the occasion.** We build a tool the morning of a class, copy it into the next day's app, and change it for that room. The best version of anything we've copied twice becomes a kit.
- **Show the work.** We keep logs of how things were made, and the record of the process is as much a deliverable as the product.
- **Keep the sources apart.** In our notes we mark which words are a person's, which are source material, and which are an AI's proposal, and we don't report a plan as something that happened.
- **Sensitive material goes through Harvard's approved routes.** See [`data-security/`](data-security/) and [`huit/`](huit/).

In 2026–27 we:
