# The talk in brief

*What We Learned Teaching Harvard Faculty to Build with AI.* The Learning Lab at the Derek Bok Center for Teaching and Learning, at the 2026 HILT Encore Event: Thursday, November 12, 2026, 12:00–1:00 PM ET, on Zoom.

The talk is the website in this repository: the slides are the Markdown in `content/talk/`, and `content/take-home.md` lists our public repositories, workshop materials, and tools. To run it, see the root `README.md`. This page is a summary for you and your agent; the words in `content/` are the talk's own.

## The parts

1. **What is the Learning Lab?** A studio at the Bok Center whose tools were the creative industries' and whose habits come from the Writing Program. AI made each of our skills easy, which raises the question of what our edge is, and gave us a vantage point on the whole phenomenon. See [`learning-lab.md`](learning-lab.md).
2. **Why this particular AI thing?** One-hour department visits never get past the first hour. Meanwhile the quality question changed: in some fields AI can now produce work that would have passed as excellent, but getting there takes skill and steering, not one-shot prompting. So we designed a four-day intensive to bring faculty to Claude Code.
3. **How we designed it, and how the design shifted.** The first time through anything, you're designing it as much as delivering it, and the tools changed every day. The recipe-card activity (photos of old recipe cards in, a researched web gallery out) showed the maneuvers of academic work in miniature; its point of failure was that people finished it and still couldn't see how to use it for their own work, so we built recipes from their own use cases. The hardest problems were procedural: copying a path, the structure of their own files.
4. **What we learned.** Personalized learning materials emerged in the first week, unplanned, and we can now make them for students too. Sharing context became the key move in collaboration. In our course, students went from "why web tools?" in week two to building their own podcast-recording software by week eight, and constant oral reflection, with a complete record in git and the chat, starts to close the gap between formative and summative. Everyone can create their own interface for organizing their ideas.
5. **What comes next.** What's still difficult? What's still scarce?

## What we're wondering

- The just-in-time, disposable app: what does it mean when no one shares the same tool, and there's no message board for the app you vibe coded?
- Is learning a tool worth it if it changes before the learning pays off?
- Learning objectives are moving targets: what is there to get better at?
- We've built more than ever. Have we learned more than ever?

The full list, with what we learned, is in `content/wondering.md`.

## Where this kit fits

The talk ends part 4 on the idea that everyone can make their own interface for organizing their ideas, and closes with the take-home. This kit is the take-home you can open: a context folder like the ones our students share with each other, and a few scripts that call Harvard's approved AI gateways. Ask your agent to read this folder, then to help you start a context folder of your own.
