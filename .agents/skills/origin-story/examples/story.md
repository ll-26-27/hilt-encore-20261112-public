# The Proofing Drawer (a made-up example)

Ada Quill is fictional. Script written by the agent from a one-paragraph made-up brief: a lab chemist who started teaching bread baking and found that her lab habits were the thing her students needed.

## Visual direction

Warm ink-and-wash comic style, cream paper, flour-white highlights, one accent color (tomato red) for Ada's scarf. Ada: a woman in her fifties, short gray hair, round glasses, a red scarf, sleeves rolled up. Consistent face, costume, and proportions in every panel. Clean rectangular caption boxes, rounded speech bubbles, hand-lettered capitals.

## Panel 1 — The old world

Art: A tidy chemistry lab. Ada, in safety glasses and her red scarf, writes in a lab notebook beside a row of labeled flasks and a thermometer.

Caption: FOR TWENTY YEARS, ADA QUILL MEASURED EVERYTHING.

## Panel 2 — The crossing

Art: Ada stands in a community kitchen with flour on the counter, holding the same lab notebook, looking at a lumpy, collapsed loaf. Two students look worried.

Caption: THEN SHE AGREED TO TEACH A BREAD CLASS.

Dialogue: Ada: "WHAT TEMPERATURE WAS THE KITCHEN?"

## Panel 3 — The power appears

Art: Close on the counter: a row of dough bowls, each with a hand-written label card showing time and temperature, a thermometer in one bowl. Ada's hand writes on a card.

Caption: HER POWER WASN'T BAKING. IT WAS KEEPING TRACK.

## Panel 4 — The power at work

Art: The students hold up tall, golden loaves and their own small notebooks. Ada smiles behind them, scarf bright, arms crossed.

Dialogue: Student: "WE CHANGED ONE THING AT A TIME!"

Caption: NOW EVERY LOAF IS AN EXPERIMENT.

## Editorial notes

All of this is invented, as a test of the skill. The preparer leaves this section out of image prompts.
