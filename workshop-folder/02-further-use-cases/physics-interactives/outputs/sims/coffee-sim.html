Where 70 % comes from. “Done” has to be defined, because the equation approaches 100 % without ever reaching it. Brewers measure extraction yield, the fraction of the bean's dry mass that dissolves, and a well-made cup lands at 18–22 %. Roasted coffee is only about 30 % water-soluble to begin with, so a real brew removes roughly 70 % of what is available to remove. Coffee is not brewed to exhaustion; past that point it turns bitter. Both brews use the same definition, and both the 70 % and the fitted D95 cancel out of the comparison below, which is a ratio of two times.

What this model ignores

  • D95 is fitted, not measured. It was chosen so that hot brew reaches 70 % at 5 minutes with a 0.35 mm grind, so the hot row of the calibration cannot fail on its own and is not evidence for anything. The fitted value, 3.0×10−11 m²/s, is below most published effective diffusivities for coffee solubles, which cluster nearer 10−10 m²/s. Part of that is the 70 % definition: a stricter cutoff would fit a larger D, and a 95 % cutoff would put it at about 1.0×10−10. The rest is probably the single-particle assumption below. Matching brewing practice and matching the published constant pull in opposite directions here, and this sim chose brewing practice.
  • Both brews approach the same final yield. Lipids and heavier melanoidins dissolve poorly at 4 °C, so cold brew probably reaches a lower one. Each compound also has its own solubility curve, so the two brews differ in composition and not only in strength. This changes how strong cold brew gets rather than how long it takes, so it does not affect the calibration.
  • The water is treated as infinitely dilute. Cold brew is often brewed at 1:5 and diluted afterwards, against roughly 1:16 for drip. A more concentrated brew has a smaller concentration gradient and extracts more slowly. This is one plausible source of the remaining factor of 1.7.
  • One particle size. A real grind is a wide distribution. Fines finish in seconds and the largest particles never do.
  • Spheres, and no flow. Coffee particles are irregular and porous, so D here is an effective lumped value. Hot brew also has water moving through the bed, which carries solute away from the particle surface; cold brew mostly sits still.
  • Yield is not flavour. The 70 % target is a whole-cup average, and what a cup tastes like depends on which compounds came out rather than how many. The 16 hours may well be chosen for taste, which this equation says nothing about.
