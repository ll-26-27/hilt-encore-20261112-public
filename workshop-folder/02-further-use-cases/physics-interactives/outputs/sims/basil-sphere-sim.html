How thick is the shell on a basil sphere?

Basil purée is dripped into a calcium bath. Calcium ions walk inward and crosslink the alginate as they go, so the gel shell is simply however far the calcium got. Drag time in the bath and watch the wall thicken.

What this model ignores

  • The bath never runs out of calcium. In a real bath the surface concentration drops as spheres soak.
  • D is held constant. It actually falls as the gel network forms, so real shells grow slightly slower than √t.
  • No shrinkage, no syneresis, no water leaving the sphere.
  • Perfect spheres. Real ones flatten where they rest and gel unevenly there.
  • Alginate concentration is not represented at all, though it changes both D and the stiffness.
  • The specimen darkens as it gels. That is a rendering, not a measurement — real spheres do not change colour anything like this much.

Sources and calibration

δ ≈ √(2Dt) is the standard one-dimensional diffusion-front result, valid while the shell is thin compared with the radius. GENED 1104, Week 6.

D for Ca²⁺ in calcium-alginate gel is reported in the range 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻⁹ m²/s. The slider spans that range, and picking a value inside it is a judgement you have to defend rather than a constant you can look up.

The calibration. Chefs pull caviar-sized spheres at about two minutes and get a shell of a few tenths of a millimetre, which this reproduces. The claim that leaving them "a bit too long" ruins them does not survive: at these values a 5 mm sphere needs on the order of an hour to set through. Either the folklore is loose or D is higher than the published range. Resolving that is a legitimate result to report.
