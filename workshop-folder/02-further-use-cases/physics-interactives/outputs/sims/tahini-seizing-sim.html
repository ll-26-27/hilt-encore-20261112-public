What this model ignores
  • No calibration data exists for plain tahini. Published rheology papers measure tahini blended with honey, cocoa, or grape syrup — never a clean water-only sweep. The peak location (~8% water) and coordination number (Z≈6) here are borrowed from wet-sand and wet-powder literature, not measured on sesame.
  • Z(w) is a smooth, single-humped stand-in for a real transition between pendular, funicular, and capillary states — each has its own physics; this model blends them into one curve for a single equation and one slider.
  • The "seize" threshold is a design choice, not a measurement. It's set to equal the model's own cohesive stress right at the pendular→funicular boundary (w=5%) — meaning the sim defines "holds a peak" as "connected enough to reach funicular." That's a clean story to defend, but it's a modeling decision, not an independently measured gravity/paste-weight calculation.
  • Shear rate is ignored. Real tahini is shear-thinning — it seizes at rest but can still be forced to move under a strong enough stir. This model only reports a static yield stress.
  • Roast level, oil content, and particle shape all shift these constants in practice and aren't represented here.
Calibration check: no independent water-only tahini dataset was found to test this against within a factor of two — see "what this model ignores." The qualitative shape (rise, peak near the pendular→funicular boundary, fall) matches wet-granular-material literature (Richefeu et al.; Møller et al.), not tahini-specific measurements.
