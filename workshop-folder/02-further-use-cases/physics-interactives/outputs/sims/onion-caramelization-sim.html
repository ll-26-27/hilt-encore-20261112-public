What this model ignores
  • Not all burner power actually reaches the onions — some is lost to the pan, the air, and the stovetop.
  • Lv (latent heat of vaporization) is treated as constant, but it actually drops slightly as temperature rises.
  • No Maillard browning chemistry — this model only accounts for water loss, not the sugar/protein reactions that make onions taste "caramelized."
  • Water doesn't evaporate at a perfectly steady rate — early moisture loss is faster, and the last bit trapped in collapsed cell walls is slower.
  • Assumes even heat distribution across the pan; real pans have hot spots and need stirring.
  • Ignores the energy already spent heating the onions from room temperature before evaporation starts in earnest.
  • Onion color darkening here is a visual timer, not a browning model — real caramelization depends on temperature and sugar concentration, not just elapsed time.
  • The vessel converts mass to volume assuming water's density is exactly 1 g/mL.
  • "Medium onion" is a rough kitchen approximation (~150 g) — real onions vary a lot in size.
