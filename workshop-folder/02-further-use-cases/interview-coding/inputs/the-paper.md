# The paper

Deterding, Nicole M., and Mary C. Waters. 2021. "Flexible Coding of In-depth Interviews: A Twenty-first-century Approach." *Sociological Methods & Research* 50(2): 708–739. https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124118799377

- First published online: October 1, 2018. In print: 2021, volume 50, issue 2.
- Authors: Nicole M. Deterding (then at the Institute for Research on Poverty, University of Wisconsin–Madison) and Mary C. Waters (Harvard University).

## What it argues, in brief

Qualitative coding as most sociologists were taught it comes from grounded theory, which grew up in the 1960s around paper, scissors, and index cards: start with many small codes, line by line, and build up to concepts. Deterding and Waters show, from interview articles in five American Sociological Association journals (2010–2015), that today's interview studies look nothing like that: they are large (a median of 55 interviews), often team-coded, usually combined with other data, and seldom say how they were coded. They propose "flexible coding" for qualitative data analysis software: first index each transcript with broad codes taken from the interview protocol, writing memos while reading; then apply analytic codes to the indexed sections, one research question at a time; then use the software to test the resulting typology, look for negative cases and alternative explanations, and report transparently what was done. Their running example is Deterding's study of low-income mothers' plans to return to college after Hurricane Katrina.
